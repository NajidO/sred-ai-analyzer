# SR&ED Technical Project Information Draft

**Company:** Northstar Scientific Instruments Inc.
**Fiscal year:** January 1, 2025 to December 31, 2025
**Project title:** Compact Electron Microscopy Imaging Platform
**Project status:** Ongoing

## 1. What was the project trying to achieve?

We wanted to develop a smaller scanning electron microscopy platform for laboratory
and production environments. The new unit needed a shorter electron-optical column
while still producing useful high-resolution images and remaining stable after start-up.

The project involved the objective lens, pole-piece geometry, beam alignment, thermal
management, secondary-electron detector, and software compensation. A successful
design would be easier to install and operate than our existing laboratory system.

## 2. What technology did you have at the beginning?

We had an existing larger SEM design and understood the usual methods for lens-current
control, focusing, stigmation, vacuum control, beam scanning, and detector gain. We
also had access to simulation software, supplier information, and published electron
optics material.

The existing design could not simply fit into the planned enclosure. We expected that
we could shorten the column and recalibrate the electron optics.

## 3. What technical difficulty arose?

When the objective lens and scanning components were placed closer together, changes
to one part of the system affected other parts. Increasing objective-lens current
improved focus in some situations but also increased temperature and image drift.
Focus was not always repeatable after changing operating conditions.

The smaller chamber also restricted detector placement. Some detector positions
appeared to improve signal, but they sometimes affected the beam or limited the useful
working distance. We were unsure whether lens geometry, thermal control, detector
bias, shielding, and software compensation could be combined successfully.

## 4. Why was standard engineering not enough?

Published equations and supplier guidance described the individual components, but
they did not provide a complete configuration for our compact arrangement. Simulation
results were helpful, although the assembled prototypes did not always behave as
expected.

## 5. What was the first approach?

The team reduced the pole-piece gap and increased objective-lens excitation. We
expected stronger focusing to compensate for the shorter column. We also expected the
existing passive heat-transfer approach to control the additional temperature.

## 6. What work was performed?

Several pole-piece and aperture configurations were built and tested at different
lens-current and working-distance settings. The team reviewed images and adjusted the
geometry between prototypes.

Some configurations produced sharper images but were less stable. The team then added
temperature sensors, changed heat-spreading components, and tried a basic focus
correction based on temperature. Further changes were made after the correction did
not work consistently.

The detector was also moved to different positions and tested with changes to bias and
shielding. A later arrangement appeared to provide better signal on the reference
sample, although it did not behave the same way on every sample.

## 7. What results were obtained?

The later prototype produced better images than the first compact prototype and had
less visible drift after stabilization. Detector signal also improved. The team chose
one configuration for additional development, but performance was not yet consistent
across all operating conditions.

## 8. What did the team learn?

The work showed that the compact design required the lens, thermal system, detector,
and compensation software to be considered together. Some of the original assumptions
about scaling the existing design were incorrect. Further work was planned for the
following year.
